Conditions for Mortal Sin

For a sin to be classified as mortal, three conditions must simultaneously be met:

  • Grave Matter: The act itself must be serious, violating God's law or the commandments.
  • Full Knowledge: The person must know that the action is sinful and contrary to God’s law.
  • Deliberate Consent: The person must freely and willingly choose to commit the act, giving full consent of the will.

If any one of these conditions is missing, the sin is considered venial. Mortal sins destroy charity in the heart and require sacramental confession before receiving Holy Communion.

Preparatory Prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, enlighten my mind and move my heart. Help me to see my life truthfully: to recognize my sins without excuses, to repent without despair, and to receive the mercy won for me by Jesus Christ. Give me a sincere desire to love God and neighbor more faithfully. Amen.

1. The Real Presence in the Eucharist

The Teaching: The bread and wine consecrated at Mass truly and substantially become the actual Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity of Jesus Christ (Transubstantiation), rather than remaining merely symbolic representations.

2. Artificial Birth Control (Contraception)

The Teaching: As affirmed in Humanae Vitae, every marital act must remain open to the transmission of life, and the intentional use of artificial contraceptives is intrinsically disordered.

3. Inadmissibility of Abortion

The Teaching: Human life must be respected and protected absolutely from the moment of conception. Direct abortion is a grave moral evil, and formal cooperation incurs automatic excommunication.

4. Male-Only Priesthood

The Teaching: By divine constitution, the sacrament of Holy Orders can only be validly conferred upon baptized males, as affirmed definitively by Pope John Paul II in Ordinatio Sacerdotalis.

5. Exclusivity of Holy Communion

The Teaching: Receiving the Eucharist is a sign of full communion in faith, morals, and church discipline. Therefore, non-Catholics, Catholics in irregular unions, and those conscious of unconfessed mortal sin may not receive.

6. Moral Imperative on Same-Sex Activity & Marriage

The Teaching: Homosexual inclinations are not inherently sinful, but homosexual acts are intrinsically disordered, and marriage can only be validly contracted between one man and one woman.

7. Infallibility and Authority of the Papacy

The Teaching: The Pope, when speaking ex cathedra on matters of faith and morals, possesses the charism of infallibility, and Catholics owe religious submission of intellect and will to the ordinary magisterium.

8. Sacramental Confession Requirement

The Teaching: Mortal sins must be confessed to a priest in the Sacrament of Reconciliation before receiving the Eucharist, as Christ gave the Apostles the specific authority to forgive and retain sins.

9. Indissolubility of Valid Marriage & Annulments

The Teaching: A valid, consummated sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved by any human power or for any reason other than death. Divorce separates civil status, but does not end the spiritual bond.

10. Necessity of the Church for Salvation

The Teaching: While God can save those outside visible boundaries through invincible ignorance, the Catholic Church is the ordinary instrument of salvation established by Christ ("Outside the Church there is no salvation").

Treating that name as a casual filler word, a swear word, or an exclamation of frustration strips the ultimate Reality of His dignity and treats the sacred as common.

2. Love of God: Prayer, Worship, and Gratitude

“Pray without ceasing; give thanks in all circumstances.” — 1 Thessalonians 5:17–18

Occasions:

Relationships Require Communication to Survive: At its core, religion is not merely a set of rules; it is a relationship with the living God. No human relationship—whether a marriage, a close friendship, or a family bond—can survive, let alone flourish, without regular, intentional communication. If you went weeks or months without speaking to your spouse or a close friend, the relationship would grow cold, distant, and eventually wither. Daily prayer keeps the lines of communication open, turning your heart toward God so that the relationship stays vibrant rather than theoretical.

Prayer Realigns the Human Will: As discussed earlier, putting our own will ahead of God’s is the root of moral failure and selfishness. Human nature naturally drifts toward self-preservation, comfort, and control. Daily prayer acts as a spiritual anchor or a compass recalibration. Taking time each day to step back, acknowledge God’s sovereignty, and listen to His voice helps quiet the noise of ego and temptation, making it easier to choose love, patience, and goodness when daily pressures mount.

Occasions:

Treating God purely as a cosmic problem-solver distorts the nature of Love

In any healthy human relationship—such as a marriage or a deep friendship—if one person only spoke to the other to demand help or ask for favors, while never expressing appreciation, affection, or acknowledging their own faults when they hurt the other person, the relationship would feel hollow and exploitative. Love requires mutuality, presence, and appreciation for the person themselves, not just what they can do for us.

3. Love of Neighbor: Charity, Mercy, and Forgiveness

“Let all that you do be done in love.” — 1 Corinthians 16:14

Occasions:

What is concrete love? To love someone concretely means moving love out of the realm of abstract feelings, good intentions, or polite sentiments and rooting it in tangible, real-world action.

In both theological and practical terms, concrete love refuses to stay theoretical. It takes flesh, time, and effort. As the biblical phrase puts it, it is a love demonstrated "in deed and in truth" rather than just "in word or speech" (1 John 3:18).
